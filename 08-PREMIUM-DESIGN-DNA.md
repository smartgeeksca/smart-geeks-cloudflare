# Premium Design DNA Report

The brief named fifteen reference products: Linear, Raycast, Stripe, Arc Browser, Vercel, Resend, Notion, Apple, Clerk, Supabase, Framer, Attio, Mercury, Ramp, Perplexity. Rather than list surface traits ("dark mode," "gradients," "sans-serif type") this report identifies the small number of *structural* habits that recur across nearly all of them — the habits that actually transfer to a completely different kind of business, since Smart Geeks cannot and should not look like a SaaS dashboard.

### 1. One idea per section, sized to its importance
Stripe's homepage doesn't give its payments API and its tax-compliance product the same box size — the thing they want you to understand first is bigger, first, and alone. Linear does the same with its core issue-tracker versus its secondary features. The habit isn't "big hero text," it's *unequal sizing as a signal of what matters*. **Applied to Smart Geeks (V4):** the repair-intelligence section and the buy/sell/trade section are now sized and composed completely differently from each other and from the device grid, instead of every section being "heading + row of equal boxes."

### 2. Density over decoration
Notion and Linear pages read fast because each section says one real thing and stops — they don't pad a thin idea across a full-width section with a big illustration to make it feel substantial. **Applied:** V4 merges the old "why us" section and "how it works" section (two thin sections making the same underlying point) into one repair-intelligence split that says it once, with both halves visible at the same time.

### 3. Grouped taxonomy, not flat lists
Attio and Mercury both present their feature/account categories as *grouped* clusters with a label over each group, never as one long undifferentiated list — grouping itself is information. **Applied:** the 8 device-repair categories are now presented as 3 labeled clusters (PC & Windows / Apple hardware / Peripherals & consoles) plus one cross-cutting capability band, instead of one flat grid of 9.

### 4. Real specificity over reassurance copy
Perplexity and Raycast lean on showing the actual thing (a real answer, a real command palette) rather than telling you it's fast/smart/powerful. Vague reassurance ("we're the best!") is the single most template-feeling habit in local-business marketing. **Applied:** Smart Geeks' honest, already-existing specificity — an exact street address, a real 4-city service area, an honest "we'll tell you when a repair isn't worth it" line — is now given the visual weight a SaaS company would give a real product screenshot, via the statement band and the mono "readout" treatment on the local-authority section, instead of sitting in the same generic paragraph style as everything else.

### 5. Confident restraint in copy length
Arc's and Clerk's marketing copy is short — a sentence, not a paragraph, per idea. **Applied:** the brand-story statement band takes one real, already-approved sentence and gives it the entire visual weight of a section, rather than asking a visitor to read a paragraph to get the same idea.

### 6. Asymmetric, alternating rhythm down the page
Framer and Apple's product pages alternate which side of the page carries the "big" column section to section, so scrolling has a rhythm instead of feeling like a stack of identical rows. **Applied:** V4's new `.split-editorial` grid does exactly this — wide-left in the repair-intelligence section, wide-right in the buy/sell/trade section, wide-left again in the local-authority section.

### 7. One signature surface, reused deliberately
Resend and Supabase both have a specific dark, technical surface treatment (grid lines, glow, mono type) that shows up in more than one place on the page, so it reads as the brand's surface rather than a one-off hero background. This was already identified and implemented in the V3 pass (the shared `--mesh-bg` recipe) and V4 keeps it — it was never the part of the previous critique that was wrong.

**What V4 deliberately did NOT copy from the reference set:** none of these products are local, in-person, appointment-and-drop-off service businesses, so V4 does not adopt SaaS conventions that would actively work against Smart Geeks — no fake product screenshot, no invented "trusted by 500+ customers" social-proof bar, no pricing table. The device-ecosystem and local-authority treatments exist specifically because Smart Geeks' real differentiators (device breadth, a physical Surrey address) are not things a SaaS reference site has an equivalent for.
