# V4 Brutal Design Critique — the Structural Problem (not the Surface One)

Written against the site as it stood after the V3 pass: good tokens, good motion discipline, a real dark signature surface — and still, underneath all of that, the exact same page skeleton every repair-shop template uses: hero, then a row of "why us" cards, then a row of process steps, then a flat grid of every service offered, then a generic two-column "service area" block, then FAQ, then CTA. V3 repainted that skeleton. It never questioned it. This is the critique of the skeleton itself.

**What Linear's team would say:** "Count your sections. Every one of them is the same shape: heading, one sentence, a grid of equal-sized boxes. That's not an information hierarchy, that's a spreadsheet with rounded corners. Linear's homepage has maybe two moments that use a uniform grid at all — everything else is a specific, custom-composed argument. Your service list — 9 completely different repair categories — was presented with the exact same visual weight as your 4 'why choose us' bullet points. A MacBook logic-board repair and a printer paper jam are not equally important facts about your business, but your layout insisted they were, by giving them identical boxes."

**What Vercel's team would say:** "Where's the asymmetry? Every section in the old layout was a single centered column or a uniform grid. Real editorial confidence shows up as *unequal* treatment — one thing gets to be big because it matters more, and something else gets to be small and dense because it's supporting detail. Your buy-sell-trade line of business is a genuinely distinct thing your competitors mostly don't offer, and it was buried as the 9th card in a grid of 9 identical cards. That's not a visual bug, that's an information-architecture bug — the layout was actively hiding your differentiator."

**What Apple's team would say:** "Too many sections doing too little each. Diagnosis-before-repair and your 4-step process are the same idea told twice, once as 'why us' and once as 'how it works' — two separate, thin sections instead of one section that actually earns its scroll. Density of meaning per section is what makes a page feel edited rather than assembled."

**The actual verdict:** the previous critique (V3) was right that the surface didn't feel premium. This one is different: even with a premium surface, *the section-by-section shape of the page* is what makes a visitor's eye recognize "template" almost instantly, before they've read a word — because they've seen this exact rhythm (hero → card row → card row → card row → FAQ → CTA) on a hundred other local-business sites, regardless of what color scheme sits on top of it. That rhythm is what V4 actually changes: fewer, denser, unequal sections, in a different order, several of which (the merged repair-intelligence split, the grouped device-ecosystem taxonomy, the promoted buy/sell/trade chapter) did not exist in any previous version of this site.
